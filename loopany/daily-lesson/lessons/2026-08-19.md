### Concept

Rustlings compiles the **whole file**, `main` included — so a broken `main` fails an exercise
whose tested function is perfect. Yours has two.

```rust
fn fruit_basket(basket: &mut HashMap<Fruit, u32>) { ... }
```

The `&mut` in the signature is only half the contract; the **caller** has to hand over a
mutable borrow to match:

```rust
let mut basket = HashMap::from_iter(content);  // 1. the binding is mut
fruit_basket(&mut basket);                     // 2. the call lends it out
```

Three things move together — `let mut`, `&mut` at the call, `&mut` in the signature. Yours
passes `basket` by value, so the types don't line up.

Second: `fruit_basket` returns nothing, i.e. `()`. So `let fruit_basket = fruit_basket(basket)`
binds `()`, and `println!("{fruit_basket}")` asks Rust to Display a `()`, which it can't.

## Track B · DSA

### Concept

Bitboards are banked — on to how an engine actually **chooses** a move.

A position is a node. Its children are the positions after each legal move. One move deep is
1 ply, the reply is 2 ply.

Score every leaf from **White's** point of view: positive is good for White. Then walk back up.
White maximises that number, Black minimises it — so at a White node take the **largest** child,
at a Black node the **smallest**. That alternation is the whole of minimax.

```
              White (max)              -> max(-1, +2) = +2
             /            \
      Black (min)        Black (min)    -> min(+3,-1)=-1   min(+5,+2)=+2
      /      \           /      \
    +3       -1        +5       +2
```

Note what happened to `+5`. It's the best leaf on the board and White never gets it, because
reaching it means letting Black choose — and Black answers `+2`. A node is worth what your
*opponent* will allow, not the best thing under it.

Same trap as the example: `+7` and `+6` are the juiciest leaves and neither is reachable. B3
looks promising until you notice Black can hold it to `0`, while B1's *worst* case is still
`+2`. Minimax picks the branch with the best floor, not the best ceiling.
