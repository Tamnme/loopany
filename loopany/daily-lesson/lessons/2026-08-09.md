<details>
<summary>Worked answer — open only after you've written yours</summary>

**Q1.** Any number of `&`, or exactly one `&mut`, never both at once. Many readers or one
writer.

**Q2.** It allocates a new, larger buffer (typically double), copies the 4 elements over,
frees the old one, then writes. That copy is `O(n)`, but it happens on 1 push in `n`, so the
amortized cost stays `O(1)`.

**Q3.** About 20 times (`log2(1_000_000) ≈ 19.9`) — `O(log n)`. Halving is the same move a
BST search makes at every node, which is why a balanced tree costs its height.

**Q4.** Linked list: insert or remove at a known position — `O(1)`, just rewire two
pointers; the `Vec` shifts every later element, `O(n)`. `Vec`: index `v[i]` — `O(1)` address
arithmetic; the list has to walk `i` nodes, `O(n)`.

**Q5.** `head = 2, tail = 1`. Start `(0, 0)`; push 4 → `tail = 4`; pop 2 → `head = 2`; push
2 → `tail = (4 + 1) % 5 = 0`, then `(0 + 1) % 5 = 1`. `tail` is now *below* `head` — that is
normal, and it is why "empty" and "full" both look like `head == tail` unless you also track
a length.

</details>
