## Track B · DSA

### Concept

Held — the reminder, not the lesson. A Zobrist hash XORs one random 64-bit key per
(piece, square), plus side-to-move, castling rights and the en-passant file. XOR is its own
inverse, so a move is three or four XORs and unmake is the identical three or four.

The whole table rests on one property: **two positions are the same position if and only if
they hash the same.** Today attacks the harder direction of it.

Miss one component and two different positions collide silently.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer — open only after you've written yours</summary>

Three ways one position gets two hashes:

1. **Re-randomising the table.** Fresh keys at startup, or two threads with separate tables,
   and a saved hash means nothing. Not slower — *wrong*, silently.
2. **Including something that isn't part of the position.** Commonly the en-passant key set on
   every pawn double-step, even when no enemy pawn can capture. Both positions play the same;
   one carries an extra XOR. You never get a hit — lost work, no wrong answers.
3. **Drifting from the incremental update.** A make/unmake path that forgets one XOR (castling
   rights after a rook moves, say) leaves the hash correct by *path*, not by position. Same
   square via a different move order, different hash.

**Q2** — (3) is detectable: under `debug_assertions`, recompute from scratch every N nodes and
assert it matches the incremental hash. (1) and (2) look like a healthy engine with an
unhelpfully low hit rate.

</details>
